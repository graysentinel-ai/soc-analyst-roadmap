Module Overview

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, applications and information against threats that could compromise their security.

Security professionals identify risks, implement controls, monitor activity and investigate events that may affect an organisation.

A SOC Analyst needs to understand security concepts before interpreting alerts. This module introduces the terminology and principles used in everyday defensive security work.

Why does a SOC Analyst need security fundamentals? An alert may indicate unusual activity, but an analyst must understand the affected asset, possible threat, relevant vulnerability, security controls and available evidence before deciding how to respond.

Topic 01

The CIA Triad

The CIA triad is a foundational information security model built around three objectives: confidentiality, integrity and availability.

Confidentiality

Information should be accessible only to authorised people, systems or processes.

Example: Restricting access to employee records.

Integrity

Information should remain accurate and protected against unauthorised modification.

Example: Detecting unauthorised changes to a file.

Availability

Systems and information should be accessible when authorised users need them.

Example: Maintaining access to a business application.

Understanding Security Impact

Scenario Potential Security Impact
Unauthorised access to payroll records Confidentiality
Unauthorised modification of financial records Integrity
Business application unavailable due to an outage Availability
Ransomware encrypts business files Potential impact on availability and integrity
SOC perspective When investigating an incident, consider which security objective may be affected. One incident can affect more than one part of the CIA triad.
Topic 02

Authentication and Access Control

Organisations need to verify identities and control what authenticated users and systems are permitted to access.

Authentication

Authentication is the process of verifying an identity.

Common authentication factors include:

  • Something you know: A password or PIN.
  • Something you have: A hardware security key or authenticator device.
  • Something you are: A biometric characteristic.

Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) requires evidence from more than one distinct authentication factor. It adds protection when a single factor is compromised.

Authorisation

Authorisation determines which actions an identity is permitted to perform after its identity has been established.

Least Privilege

The principle of least privilege means giving users, applications and services only the permissions needed to perform their assigned responsibilities.

Defence in Depth

Defence in depth uses multiple layers of security controls so that the failure of one control does not automatically result in a complete security compromise.

Identity Who is requesting access?
Authentication Can the identity be verified?
Authorisation What is the identity allowed to do?
Monitoring What activity was performed?
Investigation example If an account accesses a sensitive resource unexpectedly, an analyst may review authentication records, account privileges, access logs, device information and the surrounding activity.
Topic 03

Common Cybersecurity Threats

A threat is a potential cause of harm to a system, organisation or information asset.

Threats can involve malicious actors, software, human error, environmental events or other conditions that may affect security.

Common Threat Categories

Phishing

Deceptive communication designed to persuade someone to disclose information, click a link or perform an unsafe action.

Malware

Software designed to perform harmful or unauthorised activities.

Ransomware

Malware associated with denying access to data or systems, commonly through encryption.

Credential Attacks

Attempts to obtain, guess, reuse or misuse authentication credentials.

Denial of Service

Activity that disrupts or degrades access to a service or system.

Data Exfiltration

Unauthorised transfer of information outside its intended environment.

Privilege Escalation

Obtaining permissions beyond those initially assigned to an account or process.

Insider Threat

Security risk arising from someone with authorised or trusted access.

Phishing Indicators

Examples of warning signs include:

  • Unexpected requests for credentials.
  • Unusual urgency or pressure.
  • Unexpected attachments.
  • Links whose destinations do not match the displayed text.
  • Unusual sender addresses or domains.
  • Requests that bypass normal organisational processes.

Malware Concepts

Type Description
Virus Malicious code that can attach to other files or programs.
Worm Malware capable of self-propagation across systems or networks.
Trojan Malicious software disguised as legitimate software.
Ransomware Malware used to deny access to data or systems, often through encryption.
Spyware Software that collects information without appropriate authorisation.
Rootkit Tools designed to conceal activity or maintain privileged access.
Analyst caution A suspicious email, unusual process or failed login is not sufficient by itself to prove a successful attack. Analysts must collect and correlate evidence.
Topic 04

Threats, Vulnerabilities, Exploits and Risk

These terms are related, but they describe different parts of a security problem.

Term Meaning
Asset Something valuable that an organisation wants to protect.
Threat A potential cause of harm.
Vulnerability A weakness that could be exploited or otherwise lead to harm.
Exploit A method or technique that takes advantage of a vulnerability.
Risk The potential for an adverse outcome, considered in context.
Impact The consequence if an event occurs.
Likelihood How plausible or probable an event is under the chosen assessment method.

Example Scenario

Scenario: An outdated business server

An organisation operates a server containing sensitive business information. The server runs software with a known vulnerability and has not received an available security update.

Asset: Business server and its information.

Vulnerability: Unpatched software weakness.

Threat: An actor capable of exploiting the weakness.

Potential impact: Unauthorised access or service disruption.

Risk: Depends on exposure, exploitability, existing controls and business impact.

Risk Assessment Fundamentals

Organisations use different risk assessment methods. A simplified approach considers likelihood and impact.

Likelihood Impact Illustrative Risk
Low Low Lower
Medium Medium Moderate
High High Higher
Low High Requires contextual assessment

This is a conceptual example, not a formal risk scoring standard. Actual risk ratings should follow the organisation's approved methodology.

Vulnerability Severity

The Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) is a framework used to communicate the severity characteristics of software vulnerabilities.

A CVSS score is useful context, but it does not represent the complete business risk of a vulnerability.

SOC perspective A vulnerability alert becomes more meaningful when analysts understand the affected asset, exposure, available patches, active exploitation evidence and business context.
Topic 05

Security Controls

Security controls are safeguards used to reduce security risks and protect organisational assets.

Control Categories by Function

Preventive Controls

Designed to prevent or reduce the likelihood of unwanted activity.

Examples: MFA, firewalls and access restrictions.

Detective Controls

Designed to identify events or activity that may require investigation.

Examples: SIEM alerts and endpoint monitoring.

Corrective Controls

Designed to correct identified issues or restore a secure state.

Examples: Patching and configuration correction.

Recovery Controls

Support restoration after disruption or an incident.

Examples: Tested backups and recovery procedures.

Controls in a SOC Environment

Control Primary Purpose
Multi-Factor Authentication Reduce unauthorised account access.
Endpoint Protection Help identify and respond to endpoint threats.
Firewall Enforce network traffic policies.
Centralised Logging Collect records for monitoring and investigation.
Security Monitoring Identify activity requiring investigation.
Backups Support data and system recovery.
Patch Management Address known software weaknesses.
Important distinction A security control is not effective merely because it exists. Its configuration, coverage, operation and validation matter.
Topic 06

Indicators of Compromise and Attack

Security analysts use indicators and observations to identify activity that may be related to a threat.

Indicator of Compromise (IoC)

An Indicator of Compromise is an observable artefact that may suggest a system or environment has been compromised.

Indicator Example
File Hash A known malicious file hash.
IP Address An address associated with known malicious infrastructure.
Domain A domain linked to a reported malicious campaign.
URL A known malicious web resource.
File Path A file location associated with a specific investigation.

Indicator of Attack (IoA)

An Indicator of Attack describes observable behaviour that may indicate an attempted or ongoing attack.

Behaviour Why It May Matter
Repeated authentication failures May indicate password guessing or another authentication issue.
Unusual privilege changes May indicate an unexpected change in access.
Unexpected process execution May require investigation of the process and its parent.
Unusual outbound connections May require investigation of destination and application context.

IoC vs IoA

IoC IoA
Focuses on observable compromise artefacts. Focuses on suspicious behaviour or attack activity.
May include hashes, domains and IP addresses. May include unusual execution or authentication patterns.
Can support matching and enrichment. Can support behavioural investigation and detection.
Context is essential Indicators can become outdated, be reused by legitimate services or produce false positives. Validate their source, age, relevance and supporting evidence before making a security decision.
Topic 07

Introduction to MITRE ATT&CK

MITRE ATT&CK is a knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques based on observed behaviours.

It helps security professionals describe and organise adversary activity using a common framework.

Core Concepts

Tactic

Describes the adversary's objective or reason for performing an action.

Technique

Describes a method used to achieve an objective.

Sub-technique

Provides a more specific description of a technique.

Detection

Describes approaches that may help identify relevant adversary behaviour.

Example ATT&CK Tactics

Tactic General Objective
Initial Access Gain an initial entry point into an environment.
Execution Run malicious or unauthorised code.
Persistence Maintain access over time.
Privilege Escalation Obtain higher permissions.
Defense Evasion Avoid or weaken security detection.
Credential Access Obtain account credentials.
Discovery Gather information about an environment.
Collection Gather information of interest.
Exfiltration Transfer data outside the environment.
Impact Disrupt, damage or manipulate systems and data.

Why SOC Analysts Use ATT&CK

  • Describe observed behaviour consistently.
  • Organise threat investigation findings.
  • Understand possible stages of an intrusion.
  • Identify detection opportunities.
  • Communicate technical findings to other security teams.
Important ATT&CK mapping is a way to describe observed behaviour. It is not proof that a specific threat actor or attack campaign is responsible for an event.
Hands-on Learning

Practical Exercises

These exercises use fictional scenarios, public references and synthetic information. Do not test real systems or investigate real people without appropriate authorisation.

Exercise 01

Classify the CIA Triad

Objective: Identify which security objective is affected by a scenario.

Scenarios:

  1. An unauthorised person reads a confidential employee file.
  2. An attacker modifies a financial record without permission.
  3. A business application becomes unavailable during an outage.
  4. An unauthorised change affects the accuracy of a database.
  5. A service interruption prevents users from accessing a critical application.

Tasks:

  1. Identify the relevant CIA objective for each scenario.
  2. Explain the reason for your classification.
  3. Identify one security control that may reduce the risk.

Evidence: CIA triad classification worksheet.

Exercise 02

Classify Security Threat Scenarios

Objective: Recognise common cybersecurity threats.

Scenarios:

  1. An employee receives a fake password-reset email.
  2. A business workstation displays a ransom demand.
  3. An application receives an unexpected volume of requests and becomes unavailable.
  4. An account repeatedly attempts to access resources outside its normal permissions.
  5. A sensitive file is transferred to an unauthorised external destination.

Tasks:

  1. Identify the possible threat category.
  2. List the evidence available in the scenario.
  3. List the information that is still missing.
  4. Suggest an appropriate defensive investigation step.

Evidence: Threat classification report.

Exercise 03

Threat, Vulnerability and Risk Analysis

Objective: Separate security concepts within a scenario.

A fictional organisation operates a business server with outdated software. The server contains sensitive records and is accessible through an approved business network.

Tasks:

  1. Identify the asset.
  2. Describe the potential vulnerability.
  3. Identify a plausible threat.
  4. Explain a possible exploit scenario at a high level.
  5. Describe potential business impact.
  6. Identify existing information needed to assess risk.
  7. Suggest appropriate security controls.

Evidence: Risk analysis worksheet.

Exercise 04

Identify Indicators in a Synthetic Dataset

Objective: Identify observations that may require security investigation.

Review the fictional records below:

2026-01-12 09:10:01 | user=alex | event=login_success 2026-01-12 09:12:15 | user=alex | event=login_failed 2026-01-12 09:12:19 | user=alex | event=login_failed 2026-01-12 09:12:23 | user=alex | event=login_failed 2026-01-12 09:13:02 | user=alex | event=login_success 2026-01-12 09:14:40 | user=alex | event=file_access 2026-01-12 09:15:12 | user=alex | event=admin_action

Tasks:

  1. Identify activity that deserves further review.
  2. Build a timeline of the events.
  3. Identify what the records establish.
  4. List at least four pieces of missing context.
  5. Explain why the records alone do not prove compromise.
  6. Write a short investigation summary.

Evidence: Synthetic event analysis report.

Exercise 05

Map Behaviour to MITRE ATT&CK

Objective: Practise describing security behaviour using ATT&CK concepts.

Tasks:

  1. Open the official MITRE ATT&CK website.
  2. Review the tactic descriptions.
  3. Select three fictional behaviours from your previous exercises.
  4. Identify potentially relevant ATT&CK tactics or techniques.
  5. Record the reasoning behind each mapping.
  6. Clearly label uncertain mappings.

Evidence: ATT&CK mapping worksheet with source references.

Exercise 06

Prepare a Basic Threat Observation Report

Objective: Communicate an investigation observation clearly.

Prepare a report using this structure:

THREAT OBSERVATION REPORT 1. Summary 2. Scenario Description 3. Affected Asset 4. Observed Indicators 5. Supporting Evidence 6. Potential Security Impact 7. Investigation Hypothesis 8. Missing Information 9. Recommended Next Steps 10. Limitations

Evidence: Completed threat observation report.
